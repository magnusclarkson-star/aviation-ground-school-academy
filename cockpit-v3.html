      Aircraft controls — touch, drag or tap

      Yoke or joystick

      Choose the control style for this aeroplane. Both use the same pitch and roll model, so you can compare their handling on a touchscreen.

      Yoke: turn left or right to bank; push forward or pull back to pitch.

      Engine throttle

      Change engine power. The airspeed and vertical-speed indications respond over time.

      Throttle and trim are the active controls; instrument faces cannot be adjusted.

      Rudder pedals

      Press and hold for yaw and slip/skid indication.

      On a keyboard, use A and D for rudder.

      Flap selector

      Select 0°, 10°, 20° or 30°. Flaps change lift and drag; the attitude effect below is a configurable type-dependent teaching illustration, not a universal aircraft response.

      Watch the artificial horizon for a small pitch shift as flaps travel. The sign and amount vary by aircraft and must be verified from its POH/AFM.

      What should I observe?

      Move the yoke or throttle and watch the instruments respond. The miniature aircraft on the attitude indicator stays fixed while the horizon moves.

      How aircraft movement is represented

      Pitch and bank change the horizon; throttle changes the tendency to accelerate; pitch and power influence rate of climb; bank and rudder influence heading. Forward travel changes ground-track distance.

      Important: pulling the yoke back does not mean the aircraft flies backward. Airplanes normally continue moving forward through the air while pitch changes; reverse movement is not simulated here.

      V3 roadmap and training boundaries

      This first cockpit build establishes the shared flight-state model, analogue instrument markings and touch-operated PFD pages. Real aircraft-specific dial markings, avionics procedures, calibration, failure modes and rotorcraft systems require separate, verified modules. It is educational software, not a flight training device approved for flight-credit or operational decisions.
