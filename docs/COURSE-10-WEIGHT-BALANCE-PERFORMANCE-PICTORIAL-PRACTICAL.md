## Fictional loading arithmetic — **not aircraft data**

The deliberately **invented** beam model is *not* the weight, balance or mass envelope of any real Cessna, Robinson, military aircraft or simulator representation. Units are classroom kilograms and metres.

| Imaginary station | Mass | Arm |
|---|---:|---:|
| Base classroom mass | 300 kg | 1.20 m |
| Fixed forward classroom load | 50 kg | 1.50 m |
| Adjustable rear classroom load | 0, 25, 50, 75, 100, 125 or 150 kg | 2.60 m |

- Classroom moment: `mass (kg) × arm (m)`, in `kg·m`.
- Classroom total moment: `435 + 2.6 × rear mass`.
- Classroom total mass: `350 + rear mass`.
- Fictional `CG = total moment / total mass`.
- The *invented* mass threshold is **475 kg** and the *invented* CG band is **1.10–1.50 m**; these figures must never be interpreted as a Cessna or any other type's operating limits.
- Rear load **0 kg**: total mass 350 kg, total moment 435 kg·m, CG ≈ **1.243 m** — within both classroom conditions.
- Rear load **100 kg**: total mass 450 kg — within the made-up mass threshold — but CG ≈ **1.544 m**, outside the made-up balance band.
- Rear load **150 kg**: total mass 500 kg — beyond the made-up mass threshold — with CG also outside the made-up band.

The illustrative green shaded CG band is deliberately simple. Real approved envelopes may vary with actual mass/configuration, and actual aircraft limits require its approved manual, weighing records, equipment list and aircraft-specific arm datum.

## Performance reasoning, not operational performance data

The four selectable comparisons illustrate *general trends* only:

1. Increasing mass may increase takeoff/landing ground distance requirements and reduce climb margins.
2. Higher density altitude commonly degrades takeoff/climb performance in many conventional aircraft.
3. Tailwind may increase takeoff/landing distance compared with a headwind under otherwise comparable conditions.
4. Reduced braking can worsen stopping performance and requires specific runway-condition data.

**No takeoff speed, runway distance, climb-gradient figure, maximum permissible load, flight permission or go/no-go output** is calculated. The unanswered real-world inputs include aircraft type and configuration, approved loading envelope, environmental conditions, wind components, runway data and applicable margins. A classroom comparison is not an approved aircraft performance chart.
