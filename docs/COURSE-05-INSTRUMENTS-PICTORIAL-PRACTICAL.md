# Course 05 — Flight Instruments and Electronic Displays: pictorial practical

**Added:** 14 November 2026 · **Academic status:** `SME_REVIEW_REQUIRED` · **Module:** `instruments`

## What has been added

The Academy's original Course 05 teaching sections, in-depth chapters, source artwork, existing quizzes, cockpit models, simulator controls and certificates remain unchanged. An **illustrated instrument-reading practical** now appears under the existing Study Activity. It uses seven original picture views drawn from four identifiers in the approved Academy image atlas. Students can tap each picture to enlarge it.

| Classroom exercise | Approved image views | Learning objective |
|---|---|---|
| Conventional six-pack | `detail-03`, `overview-03` | Identify airspeed, attitude, altitude, turn, heading and vertical-speed indications |
| Pitot-static source recognition | `detail-03` | Compare pressure-derived inputs and conventional airspeed, altimeter and VSI interpretation |
| Attitude, heading and ground track | `overview-03`, `cessna-04` | Explain different kinds of directional and attitude information using a fully fictional 090°/080° classroom example |
| Fictional display disagreement | `cessna-04`, `detail-03` | Separate reported indications from unverified sensor/component diagnoses and note appropriate instructor review |

Each case uses three multiple-choice responses, feedback, expandable definitions and distinct Air Scout versus aspiring-pilot prompts. The second case includes a **three-tab conceptual sensor-source map** (Airspeed, Altimeter and Vertical Speed). These are not representations of actual aircraft wiring or approved checklists.

**Technical note:** pitot-static relationships are conventional principles and depend on instrument installation. An electronic flight display may combine air data, attitude/heading and navigation inputs with different architectures, which cannot be determined from a decorative image alone. Heading, magnetic references and track may not coincide. Flight crews must rely on aircraft-approved procedures and qualified instruction for any real inconsistency.
