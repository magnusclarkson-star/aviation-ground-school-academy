# Course 01 — Aviation Orientation & Safety Culture: illustrated practical acceptance
**Added:** 14 November 2026 · **Academic status:** instructor review required · **Scope:** existing Ground School coursebook, **only** module `orientation`

## What students now receive

The original written Course 01 remains unchanged: its three introductory sections, three extended lessons, long-form illustrated reading, original images, short quizzes and applied case are preserved.

A new compact **Safety Observation Lab** appears after the Course 01 study activity and before the existing knowledge check. It offers:

1. **Supervised briefing photograph** (`photo-01`): learners decide what to do before approaching a demonstration aircraft and distinguish a classroom visit from permission to touch or operate an aircraft.
2. **Training aircraft on apron photograph** (`photo-02`): a clearly **fictional** blocked visitor route. Learners practise stopping outside the hazard and notifying the responsible adult rather than taking an unauthorised detour.
3. **Safety-management illustration** (`detail-11`): a clearly **fictional** disagreement between a training graphic and written explanation. Learners separate observed facts from guessed causes, then request source-based correction.

All three images are **cropped views of the owner-approved original Academy teaching artwork** from `academy-original-picture-atlas.js`. The original full images, cockpit panels, models and certificate designs are not edited, replaced or published in new versions.

For every case, learners choose one of three responses. The feedback distinguishes **observed fact**, **unproven assumption**, **immediate protection** and **appropriate reporting**; it also explains why the recommended decision is safer. Scouts/Air Scouts see an adult-supervision cue, while older enthusiasts and aspiring pilots see an operations-boundary cue.

The additional **four-step fictional hazard report** has fields for observation, plausible consequence, immediate protective action and responsible reporting recipient. It provides structural feedback but does not grade or transmit the typed content. The learner can clear it at any time. Prompts explicitly prohibit real personal details and precise locations, especially for young learners.

This remains **optional, ungraded, and device-only**. It does not change module scores, alter prior achievements, grant credentials, create a real occurrence report or validate operational procedures. A fictional image annotation does not assert that the described obstruction is actually visible in a supplied photograph.

## Remaining human review and completion gates

Course 01 is **not signed off** until a qualified aviation educator and safeguarding reviewer:
- verifies terminology and the current reference editions; checks age-appropriate instructions for Scouts, accurate supervision and privacy guidance;
- inspects each original photograph in context and confirms the fictional safety scenarios cannot be misread as real hazards visible in them;
- evaluates the feedback and objectives against the requested learning level, and reviews accessibility with a keyboard/screen reader and on a real Android phone in portrait/landscape;
- evaluates an example fictional observation report and provides an instructor rubric; certifies only educational classroom suitability, not real operational safety competence;
- records formal editorial approval and a versioned correction log, and separately seeks any regulatory course approval if a course-specific approval claim is intended.
